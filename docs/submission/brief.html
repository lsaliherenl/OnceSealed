The problem

Buyers increasingly hide who retires carbon credits, while the market needs proof that each credit is retired once and that quality claims rest on evidence. In CarbonPlan's OffsetsDB (495,403 retirements, 1.59 billion tonnes, 7 registries, snapshot 2026-06-01), retired tonnes explicitly marked "not disclosed", "anonymous" or "private" rose from 0.4% up to 2018 to 17.2% in 2023-2026 (our count, a conservative lower bound). Today a buyer gets either confidentiality or public proof.

The solution: a verification and privacy layer on top of registries

Once

RetirementRegistry records retirements as serial ranges in standard lots and rejects any overlap (AlreadyRetired). No transfer or mint function: retired credits never become tokens (Verra's 2022 ban).

Sealed

Only keccak256(abi.encode(buyerId, salt, batchId, start, end)) goes on-chain. The buyer's LEI is encrypted to an auditor, who runs six checks and records Verified or Rejected on-chain once. A receipt allows selective disclosure. Commitment plus encryption, not zero-knowledge.

Evidence-backed

For 11 real Verra projects, 18 yes/no checks against five ICVCM Core Carbon Principles. Every "found" cites a paragraph; a second model cross-checks; a person decides disagreements; deterministic data checks compare the documents' figures with issuance. Report hash and method anchored on-chain.
1 · Operatorregisters a batch, retires lots for a buyer; commitment + vault hash on-chain
2 · Auditoropens the sealed vault, six checks, attests on-chain once
3 · Buyerkeeps a receipt (salts); may disclose it to anyone
4 · Anyoneverifies retirements, receipts and report hashes in the browser, against Sepolia
What is real and what is simulated. Real: Verra projects and their documents, issuance and retirement histories (OffsetsDB), the contract and its transactions on Sepolia. Simulated: serial numbers (SIM-…) and buyers (fictional companies with valid-format LEIs). Nothing is retired in a real registry. Signal, not verdict: the quality score says where documents evidence a principle; it is not an ICVCM assessment or a rating.

Check it yourself in five minutes

  1. A report matches the chain. Open once-sealed.vercel.app/projects/VCS985. "Report provenance" says Matches on-chain version N: your browser downloaded the published report, hashed it and read the contract on Sepolia. Each "found" check shows the cited paragraph and its page.
  2. Every version stays readable. On the same page, "Report versions" re-hashes each earlier version and compares it with that version on the contract.
  3. A retirement is public, the buyer is not. Open once-sealed.vercel.app/verify?q=3: serial range, commitment, audit status Verified, buyer Sealed · no name on-chain, read live from Sepolia.
  4. The contract. On Etherscan the source is verified; there is no transfer or mint function. All demo transactions are listed in deployments/11155111-seed.json.
  5. The signing screens (operator, auditor, assessor, review) hold test keys, so they run locally: python scripts/demo_up.py --local starts a throwaway chain with temporary keys (see the README's Quick start). Try retiring serials 1-1000 twice to see AlreadyRetired.

Limitations

  • Prototype: simulated serials, fictional buyers, no registry integration.
  • Privacy is against the public; the registry knows its customers (KYC). An operator who lies consistently in vault and commitment is not caught.
  • One auditor, demo key handling; several auditors and rotation are future work.
  • Privacy needs a crowd: a single buyer in a period is still exposed.
  • AI reads keyword-selected excerpts; the labelled set also chose the models, so a fresh held-out set is needed.
  • Deployment uses 15.66M gas, close to the per-transaction limit.
